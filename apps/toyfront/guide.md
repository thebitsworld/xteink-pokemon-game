# Toy Front

## Goal

Two toy armies fight over a 4x4 board of bases joined by paths. Each base is worth 1 to 3 medals, shown as dots along its top. When both armies have played all their toys, the side holding more medals wins.

## Setup

Each side has twelve toys: two each of strength 1 to 6, shuffled. You always hold three; after you play one, the next comes from your pile. Your home row is the bottom row of the board, the device's is the top row. The board layout (Meadow, River, Castle, Corners, Ridge or Bridges) is picked at random, and some bases have no path between them. Who moves first alternates from game to game.

## A turn

Play one toy from your hand on a base:

- An empty base in your home row, or an empty base joined by a path to a base you hold: it becomes yours, with the toy's strength.
- A base you hold: its strength goes up by the toy's strength (9 at most).
- An enemy base joined by a path to one you hold: an attack.

## Attacks

- A stronger toy takes the base and keeps the difference (a 5 against a 3 leaves your base at 2).
- A weaker toy wears the base down by its strength, but it stays the device's.
- Equal strengths destroy each other: the base becomes empty.

If you have nowhere to play, you must give up a toy instead. When one side runs out of toys, the other keeps playing until it runs out too.

## Reading the board

- Your bases have a thick outline; the device's bases are filled black.
- The number on a base is its strength.
- A small circle on an empty base means your selected toy can go there.
- The top line shows the medals each side holds, then how many toys each has left.

## Controls

Buttons (X3/X4): Left/Right move along a row and the side buttons Up/Down move between the board rows and your toys (the row under the board). Confirm on a toy selects it and jumps the cursor to your home row; Confirm on a base plays the toy there. Back opens the menu.

Touch (X4 Pro): tap a toy to select it, then tap a base. Tap anywhere after the game ends for the menu.

## Tips

- Grab the 3-medal bases early, but do not leave them weak next to the enemy.
- Keep some strong toys for the end: the last moves often decide who holds the big bases.
- Reinforcing a front-line base makes it hard to take; a base at 9 can only be worn down.
- An exact tie wipes a base out - a cheap way to remove a valuable enemy base.
