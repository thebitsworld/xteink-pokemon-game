# Caro

## Goal
Caro, also called Gomoku, is played on a 15x15 grid. One player marks X, the other O. Get five or more of your marks in an unbroken line - across, down or diagonally - before your opponent does.

## Rules
- X always moves first. Players take turns marking one empty cell.
- Marks never move and are never removed.
- Five or more in a row wins. A line of six or more counts too.
- If the whole board fills up with no line of five, the game is a draw.
- The last mark played is shown white on black, and so is the winning line when the game ends.

## Starting a game
- vs Device: Easy, Normal or Hard. Your wins, losses and draws against each level are shown under its button.
- Two players: take turns on the same reader.
- First move: choose whether you or the device plays X (and moves first) in your next game against the device.
- Take back a move: undoes your last move and the device's reply (one move with two players).
- Resume continues an unfinished game. A game left open is kept when you exit the app.

## Controls (X3/X4 buttons)
- Left/Right move the cursor along a row; the side buttons (Up/Down) move it between rows. The cursor wraps around the edges and starts on the last move.
- Confirm places your mark on the cursor cell.
- Back opens the menu. In the menu, Back exits the app.
- When a game is over, Confirm starts a new one at the same level.

## Controls (X4 Pro touch)
- Tap a cell to place your mark. If the cells are very small on your screen, the first tap marks the cell and a second tap on it plays.
- Tap Menu (top right) for the menu.
- After a game ends, tap the board to play again.

## Tips for beginners
- Watch for an open three: three in a row with both ends empty. If you leave it, it becomes an open four, which cannot be stopped. Block it at one end.
- A four with one open end must be blocked at once.
- The strongest attacks make two threats with one move, such as a four and an open three together, or two open threes. Your opponent can only block one.
- Keep your marks close together and near the centre; scattered marks rarely make lines.
- Start against Easy, then move up. Hard looks for forced wins by a chain of fours and threes, and sees yours coming.
