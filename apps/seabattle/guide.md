# Sea Battle

## Goal
You and the device each hide a fleet on a 10x10 grid. Take turns firing at the other's grid. The first to sink the whole enemy fleet wins.

## The fleet
Each side has five ships, each a straight line of squares, across or down:
- Carrier (5), Battleship (4), Cruiser (3), Submarine (3), Destroyer (2).
Ships never touch each other, not even at a corner.

## Rules
- You fire first. Each turn is exactly one shot, hit or miss, then the device fires one.
- A miss shows a small dot, a hit shows an X. When every square of a ship is hit it is sunk: its squares turn black.
- Because ships never touch, all the squares around a sunk ship are marked as water for you automatically.
- The status lines tell you what your shot did and where the device fired, for example "You C7: hit!" or "Device B3: miss".
- Under the big grid you see your own fleet in small (black squares are your hit ships) and the list of enemy ships; sunk ones are crossed out.

## Starting a game
- Choose vs Device: Easy, Normal or Hard. Each level keeps its own wins and losses.
- Your fleet is placed at random. Shuffle until you like the layout, then Start.
- Resume continues a battle; it is kept when you exit (a fleet still being placed is not kept).

## Controls (X3/X4 buttons)
- While placing: Left or Right shuffles your fleet, Confirm starts the battle.
- In battle: Left/Right and the side buttons (Up/Down) aim; Confirm fires at the square under the cursor.
- Back opens the menu. After a game, Confirm plays again.

## Controls (X4 Pro touch)
- While placing: tap Shuffle or Start.
- In battle: tap a square of the big grid to fire there.
- Tap Menu for the menu; after a game, tap to play again.

## Tips for beginners
- Search in a checkerboard pattern: every ship is at least 2 squares long, so it must cover one of every two squares.
- After a hit, try the four squares next to it. After a second hit, keep going along that line.
- Once a ship is sunk, its neighbours are cleared for you, so look for space elsewhere.
- Late in the game, look for gaps that are still long enough for the ships left afloat.
