# Nonogram

## Goal
Uncover a hidden picture by working out which cells of the grid are filled. The numbers beside each row and above each column tell you how the filled cells in that line are grouped.

## Reading the clues
- Each number is a run of filled cells next to each other, in order from left to right (rows) or top to bottom (columns).
- "3 1" means: a run of 3 filled cells, then at least one empty cell, then a run of 1.
- "0" means the whole line is empty.
- When a line is complete, its clue is drawn in plain (not bold) type and crossed through.

## Rules
- Fill a cell you are sure belongs to the picture. If it does not, it becomes a locked mistake (a shaded cell with a bold X) and the mistake counter goes up. A filled cell is therefore always correct.
- Mark a cell (a thin X) to remind yourself it is empty. Marks are only notes: they never count as mistakes, and you can remove them again.
- The puzzle is solved when every filled cell of the picture is found. You do not need to mark the empty cells.
- Every puzzle has exactly one solution and can be solved one line at a time, without guessing.
- The clock and your mistakes are shown at the top. The clock pauses while the menu is open.

## Levels
Easy 5x5, Medium 8x8, Hard 10x10 and Expert 12x12. Each level counts the puzzles you solved and your best time. Each new puzzle is made at random. An unfinished puzzle is kept when you exit; choose Resume.

## Controls (X3/X4 buttons)
- Left/Right move the cursor along a row; the side buttons (Up/Down) move it between rows.
- Press Confirm to fill the cell.
- Hold Confirm for about half a second to mark the cell empty (or remove the mark).
- Back opens the menu. After solving, Confirm starts the next puzzle.

## Controls (X4 Pro touch)
- Tap a cell to fill it.
- The Fill/Mark switch above the grid changes what a tap does. You can also hold a finger on a cell to mark it.
- Tap Menu for the menu. After solving, tap to start the next puzzle.

## Tips for beginners
- Start with big numbers. In a 5-cell row, a clue of 5 fills the whole line; in a 10-cell row, a clue of 7 must cover the middle 4 cells whatever its position.
- Add up a line: the numbers plus one gap between each. If that equals the line length, there is only one way to fill it.
- Mark the cells you know are empty. They make the next lines easier.
- Go back and forth between rows and columns: each new cell gives a clue for the crossing line.
