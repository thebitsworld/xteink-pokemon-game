# O An Quan

## Goal
Ô ăn quan ("mandarin square capturing") is a Vietnamese mancala game. Sow stones around the board, capture squares, and take the two mandarins. The player with more points at the end wins: a mandarin is worth 10 points, a stone 1. There are 70 points in all.

## The board
- Two rows of five stone squares. The bottom row is yours (in two-player games, the bottom player's); the top row belongs to the device or the other player.
- A mandarin square at each end. Each starts with one mandarin, drawn as a big disc, and no stones.
- Each stone square starts with 5 stones: 50 stones in all. Stones are drawn as dots; past 10 a number is shown.
- The squares of a row are named A to E from the left of the screen.

## A turn
1. Pick one of your five squares that has stones, and a direction: left or right.
2. Take all its stones and drop them one by one into the next squares that way, mandarin squares included. The board is a loop.
3. Look at the square after the last stone. If it is a stone square with stones, pick them all up and keep sowing. If it is a mandarin square that is not empty, your turn ends.
4. If it is empty and the square after it has something, you capture that square. If the square after the capture is empty again and the next has something, capture that too, and so on: empty, full, empty, full. Two empty squares in a row end the turn.
5. An empty mandarin square (its mandarin already taken) counts as an empty square.

## Special rules in this version
- You can never pick up and sow a mandarin square.
- Young mandarin (quan non): a mandarin square that still holds its mandarin with fewer than 5 stones cannot be captured. Reaching it ends your turn without a capture.
- Empty row: when all five of your squares are empty at the start of your turn, you put one of your captured stones back in each. If you have fewer than five, you borrow the rest from the other player. The debt is paid back at the end. If you come up short again while you still owe stones, there is no second loan and the game ends.
- The game ends when both mandarin squares are empty. Each player then takes the stones left on their own row.
- To stop endless games, a game also ends after 40 moves in a row without a capture, or when a row is empty and nobody has stones to put back. Then each player takes their row, and whatever is still in the mandarin squares counts for nobody.

## Playing
- vs Device: Easy, Normal or Hard. Easy plays mostly at random, Normal grabs the biggest capture it can see, Hard looks a few moves ahead. Your wins and losses are shown under each level.
- Two players: take turns on the same reader.
- Who opens alternates from game to game. A game in progress is kept when you leave the app (Resume).
- After a move, changed squares are framed, captured ones shaded, and the lines under the board say what happened.

## Controls (X3/X4 buttons)
- Left/Right move the pointer along your row (only squares with stones).
- Confirm picks the square up. Then Left sows to the left, Right sows to the right. Back puts the square down again.
- Back opens the menu. When a game is over, Confirm starts the next one.

## Controls (X4 Pro touch)
- Tap one of your squares, then tap "Sow left" or "Sow right" under the board. Tap the square again to put it down.
- Tap Menu (top right) for the menu. After a game, tap the screen to play again.

## Tips
- A full mandarin square is worth a lot: do not leave an empty square in front of it for the other player.
- Before sowing, count where the last stone lands and what follows. A move that leaves "empty, full" in front of the other player gives them a capture.
- Young mandarins are safe early on, so the first captures are usually stones.
- Keep a few stones on your row: an empty row costs you five captured stones.
